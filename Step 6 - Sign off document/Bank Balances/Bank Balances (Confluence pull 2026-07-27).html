Part A. What exists today

1. What it is

Shows the current balance of every active bank account, and lets the user drill into a single account to see its activity - deposits, checks, withdrawals and other movements - since the last bank reconciliation. It is the dashboard's cash-on-hand widget: the fastest answer to "how much money do we have right now". The SME identified it as the single most-opened widget on the dashboard.

SME Very common because it shows live bank balances; users want to see either all accounts or specific ones, sometimes want several copies of the widget (one per account), and value filtering. (Marvin)

2. Where the data comes from

From the Bank Account Management module (prior doc, to confirm with engineering / Value Labs). Balances are based on unreconciled transactions only - items already cleared through a bank reconciliation are not counted again. Each account's beginning balance is the ending balance from its last reconciliation, or an opening balance if it has never been reconciled. Exact table names are not yet documented.

Part B. Users and market

5. Personas

Shared MB Accounting personas. Bank Balances skews more heavily to the primary operator (P1) than any other widget, because checking cash is a daily task.

Persona

Relationship to this widget

P1. Bookkeeper / business administrator (primary)

Opens it constantly to see live balances before writing checks, running payroll, or approving spend. The SME's most-opened widget.

P2. Senior / executive pastor (oversight)

Wants the quick "do we have money" read, not the transaction detail.

P3. Treasurer / finance committee (governance)

Cares whether there is enough available cash to operate, and how balances trend - a board-level question.

P4. External bookkeeper / accountant (contextual)

Uses it to sanity-check balances against the bank and the books.

7. Context scenarios

A. Daily cash check - bookkeeper opens the dashboard first thing to see balances across accounts. B. "Can we cover this?" - before a large check or payroll run, checks the relevant account. C. Reconciling - looks at what has moved in an account since the last reconciliation. D. Board question - treasurer asks how much the church has; the honest answer needs available (unrestricted) cash, not just the bank total. E. Multi-account / HQ - an org with many accounts or entities wants per-account instances and a global company switch. F. Overdrawn account - an account goes negative; it must be obvious, not hidden from the chart.

8. Competitor and analog benchmark

Desktop research Church/nonprofit accounting tools (Aplos, Realm/ACS, PowerChurch, ParishSOFT, QuickBooks Nonprofit) all surface cash and fund balances, and the market convention is to show available cash by fund, not only the raw bank total. On visualisation: a pie is only appropriate with a handful of slices and one dominant share, cannot show negatives, and is poor for comparison - a sorted horizontal bar or the table is the better default for multiple accounts (Domo, CleanChart, Eval Academy).

8b. The key domain fact: a bank balance is not spendable cash

Desktop research In church finance, not all cash is spendable cash: a healthy-looking bank balance can hide that much of the money is restricted or reserved (building fund, missions, designated gifts). Separate bank accounts are not the same as separate funds - a church typically holds several funds in one account and tracks restrictions in the books. So the total this widget shows can overstate what is actually available to spend, which is exactly the number a bookkeeper or treasurer needs. Sources: Kleer, Grain / ParishSOFT, Nonprofit Accounting Basics, Ministry Brands.

Part C. Designer brief

9. The brief

The problem the design must solve. Read with Section 10 (decisions/trade-offs). Design ideas are options with reasoning, not instructions.

9.1 Preserve: live balances across all accounts; the all-accounts and single-account reads; refresh preserving the selection; the ability to focus one account.

9.2 Widget states

State

What the user should get

No bank accounts / no data

A purposeful empty state, not a blank pie. To confirm live

No module / no rights

Hidden or an explicit "no access". Entitlement detection unknown. To confirm @Oisin Curran @Feargal Phelan

Negative / overdrawn account

Must be obvious in every view, including any chart. Verified live 23 Jul 2026: the table shows negatives with a minus sign; today no pie is present in the All-Accounts view.

Loading / error

Loading state; on error a message + retry, never a stale or empty balance that reads as real.

9.3 Constraints

Constraint

Meaning

Unreconciled-only basis

The balance reflects unreconciled activity since the last reconciliation, not the live bank-statement balance. Label it so users do not misread it.

Bank balance is not available cash

Fund restrictions mean the total can overstate spendable cash (8b). At minimum do not imply the total is spendable; ideally surface available/unrestricted alongside it. Verified live 23 Jul 2026: the widget shows ending balance only, no available split.

Charts must handle many accounts and negatives

A pie breaks with many accounts and cannot show negatives; prefer a sorted bar or the table as the default (8).

Accessibility (Pathway AA)

Values readable as text, not hover- or colour-only; name any icon controls; outflows (checks/withdrawals) clearly labelled as negative even when drawn as positive bars.

Localisation

Currency/date must follow the org. Now Verified live 23 Jul 2026 on Bank Balances itself: £ (GBP) shown for the US org "1 Saint Michael Church"; should be $.

Company scoping

Company is global product context; support multiple widget instances for multi-account orgs (SME).

10. Significant design decisions

10.1 The two-view switch is unusually heavy. The dropdown swaps the entire layout and chart type between All Accounts and Single Account. Consider expressing these as the shell's own views (or a focus overlay for a single account) rather than a mode hidden in a dropdown, so the user knows which view they are in and can get back.

10.2 Rethink the All-Accounts pie. It is the wrong default when there are many accounts or any negatives, and it silently hides overdrawn accounts. Prefer a sorted horizontal bar (or the table) as the primary comparison, with the total shown explicitly; use a pie only for a handful of accounts with one dominant share. Never drop negatives without showing them somewhere. Live re-verified 23 Jul 2026: the All-Accounts view on beta1 is already a plain two-column table with no chart, so the pie may already be gone or data/size-dependent; confirm across orgs and on the Single Account view.

10.3 Label the balance basis. Make it clear the figure is the balance since the last reconciliation (unreconciled activity), not the cleared bank-statement balance. Oisin's own open question flags this; consider an option to view all items.

10.4 Surface available vs total cash. The biggest conceptual gap: the widget answers "what is in the accounts", but the operator's real question is "what can we spend". Explore showing unrestricted/available cash (from fund balances) alongside the raw bank total, or clearly framing the total as pre-restriction. Needs the fund data - a product/data question.

10.5 Multiple instances + saved default account. Serve the SME's "several copies, one per account" by allowing duplicate instances in the shell and remembering each instance's selected account.

10.6 Outflow labelling. In the Single Account bar chart, Checks and Withdrawals are stored negative but drawn positive; label them explicitly as outflows so the chart is not misread.

10.7 Drill inside the widget. The SME wants to click an account/line and see detail. Serve it in a focus overlay, respecting rights, rather than leaving the dashboard.
