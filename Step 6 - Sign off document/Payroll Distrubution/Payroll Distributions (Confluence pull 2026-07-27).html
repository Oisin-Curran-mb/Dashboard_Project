Part A. What exists today

1. What Payroll Distributions is

The widget shows a breakdown of payroll spend by distribution across a chosen date range: how the total paid splits across the org's distribution lines, for any period the user picks. Its grain is the distribution summary (one row per distribution), not a raw list of individual checks. Confirmed live on beta1: real rows render as org-defined labels, such as "AA Aid" and "Administration Staff".

What "distribution" actually is (verified live, 20 Jul 2026, Compensation Information walk). The payroll setup has three distinct levels, and it is easy to conflate them:

  • Compensation is the top object, set up under Payroll > Modify > Compensation Information. It is org-named (for example "AA Meeting Council", "CellPhone Allowance-Nonaccountable"). A compensation can have one or more distributions, or none.

  • Distribution is a named line inside a compensation, mapped to a general-ledger account (account structure + account number). It is org-named (for example "AA Aid" inside "AA Meeting Council", or "cell allowance" inside the cellphone compensation). This is the grain the widget's table shows: the live rows "AA Aid" and "Administration Staff" are distributions.

  • Pay type is a standard, fixed pick-list attribute on each distribution (Regular Pay, Overtime, Doubletime, Holiday, Other, Vacation, Sick, Personal, Misc., Social Security, Medicare, and so on). Unlike compensations and distributions, pay types are the same across every org.

So the widget groups by distribution (org-defined, GL-mapped), not by "compensation type" and not by pay type. Pay type is a separate standardized dimension that sits underneath every distribution (see 10.9).

What the grand total means. The total is the sum of every distribution paid within the selected date range, for the current company, across all distributions. It is not a single line, and it is not an "as of today" balance: it is money that flowed out over a span of time. With the default range (1 January to today) it reads as total payroll paid year to date; narrow the range to a month and it reads as total paid that month. The distribution rows are the breakdown; the grand total is their sum. This flow-not-balance nature is the root of the most important design decision on this page (Section 10).

Desktop research Why the breakdown matters. The split is what makes this widget meaningful rather than a bare total, because in church payroll the way pay is categorised carries the tax treatment. Ordained ministers have a dual tax status (employee for income tax, self-employed for Social Security and Medicare), a designated housing allowance is income-tax-excluded but SECA-liable and lands in W-2 Box 14, and lines differ in what they are subject to. So a distribution or pay type is not just a label, it signals how that pay is taxed and reported, which is why an administrator reasons about payroll by these breakdowns. This is context for why the grain is right; it is not a task the user performs here (there are no tax jobs in Section 6). Sources: IRS Topic 417, IRS housing allowance, ChurchShield, Auxilio.

2. Where the data comes from

Original documentation, validated against Shelby's own dashboard reference. The widget totals the amount per distribution for checks issued within the selected date range. Voided checks and certain internal check types are excluded, so figures reflect only real, valid payments. There is no cache: every load or refresh queries live payroll records. Note for later decisions: because PRHistory stores the actual check date of every processed payroll, the real pay dates an org has already run are available in the data.

Part B. Users and market

5. Personas

Desktop research and SME interview. The primary persona is Ministry Brands Accounting's primary persona; payroll is run at the individual church, not at a headquarters.

Persona

Relationship to this widget

P1. Church bookkeeper or business administrator (primary)

Runs payroll, often part time or wearing several hats. Needs to see what was paid, in what categories, and whether it looks right. Frequently not a payroll specialist, so clergy tax complexity is a burden they carry, not an expertise they hold (ChurchShield). This is Ministry Brands Accounting's primary persona.

P2. Senior/lead pastor or executive pastor (oversight)

Reviews total comp cost and its distribution for budgeting and board reporting. Wants the "where did payroll go" answer without reading a register. Thinks in calendar and fiscal terms (year to date, this year), not pay runs.

P3. Treasurer / finance committee (governance)

Volunteer oversight. Uses category totals to sanity-check budget vs actual and to answer board questions. Often relies on reports rather than logging in, so access for this persona is partly a licensing question.

P4. External bookkeeper or payroll service (contextual)

Some churches outsource. When they do, this widget is a quick internal check against what the service reports.

Note the split that matters for design: P1 thinks operationally (by pay run, "did the last payroll look right"), while P2 and P3 think in calendar and fiscal periods ("what have we spent this year"). The period control has to serve both mindsets. This is central to Section 10.

7. Context scenarios

Desktop research and SME interview, grounded in the domain and verified behavior. Each ends with how it plays out today.

A. Just after a pay run. The bookkeeper runs the monthly payroll and opens the dashboard to confirm the total and that housing allowance landed in its own line. Today: the widget shows the distributions if the dashboard is on the right company and the range covers the run; there is no quick period control, so they adjust dates by hand.

B. Budget season. The executive pastor wants salary vs benefits vs housing, year to date. Today: this year's split is available once the range is set to year to date.

C. A number looks wrong. Benefits look high. The bookkeeper wants to see which employees or checks drove it. Today: no drill from the widget, and Earnings Inquiry only answers per named employee, so they go employee by employee or run a report.

D. Multi-entity org. A church that also runs a school and a daycare wants payroll for just the school's pay group. Today: all pay groups are combined; no way to isolate one. (SME interview, 8b; pay group deferred per 8c and 10.4.)

E. Board meeting prep. The treasurer needs a defensible by-line payroll total. Today: they read the widget and retype the figures into their board doc; no export.

F. New or payroll-free church. A church that does not run payroll here, or a brand-new setup, selects the widget and sees "No records to display" with a blank total. Today: no explanation of what the widget is or how to populate it.

8c. Pay groups: what we found, and why the picture is not yet clear enough to design against

Verified live (95301 and beta1) and confirmed by Oisin Curran (page comments, 16 Jul). This section documents what we could and could not establish about pay groups, because it determines whether a pay-group filter (JTBD 4, 10.4) can responsibly be designed this pass.

What we established:

  • Pay groups exist and are set up under Payroll > Modify > Employer Information, as a named list (the defaults "Group A" through "Group E" on the test company). On that screen a pay group is just a name among other employer settings; it does not visibly carry its own pay frequency or schedule. Verified live

  • The multi-entity need is real and specific: an org running, say, a daycare, a school, and a church would use pay groups to separate them, and wants to read one entity's distributions without the others mixed in (SME, 8b). For a single-entity church (everyone in one group, or the A–E defaults untouched) a pay-group filter adds nothing.

What we could not resolve, and this is the problem: pay group is a grouping of employees (Employer Information), which is a different axis from the pay-classification chain the widget uses (compensation, distribution, pay type, see 10.9). Pay group is not the same as any of those. On the individual employee record there is no field plainly labelled "pay group", so how an employee actually maps to a pay group is not something we could pin down from the UI in this pass. We also could not confirm whether an employee can belong to more than one group, or, critically, whether a historical distribution record (PRHistory / PRHistoryCompensation) even carries a pay-group key at all. Oisin flagged this last point as one for Value Labs, noting the pay/hours breakdown is stored but not whether the pay-group link sits on the distribution table or elsewhere.

Why this matters for design: a filter is only as sound as the data model under it. We cannot design a pay-group filter, decide whether it is one selector or several, whether it composes with the date range, what it should return when an employee has moved groups mid-year, without first understanding how a pay group attaches to a distribution. Getting that understanding is not a quick lookup; it is a proper investigation into the payroll data model, and it needs someone who can see the schema, i.e. Value Labs. That is significant effort, disproportionate to bolting one optional filter onto one widget unless the multi-entity need is prioritised in its own right.

9.3 Constraints

Constraint

What it means

Widget views (established shell)

The widget lives in the platform's existing set of views — a content-depth vector, not a size vector: glance, detail, explore. Any view may span 3, 4, 6, or 12 columns of the 12-column grid depending on screen width, breakpoint, and the view itself, so "view" describes content depth, not a fixed footprint. There is also a focus state: an isolate-and-focus overlay (the widget expanded in place over the dashboard), not a fourth view and not navigation to another page. This is a made and built decision, not a question; it is the material to design within. The source of truth for the views is the widget shell component in the Pathway master file: widget shell (Figma). See 10.8 for a suggested content mapping across the views.

Clergy tax categories are first-class

Housing allowance, SECA allowance, and the clergy/lay split are meaningful groupings, not arbitrary line items. Line labels must be the org's own, shown faithfully.

Distributions are org-defined and variable; pay type is standard

Distribution names and counts come from the org's own setup and differ per church; never assume a fixed list. Confirmed live: "AA Aid", "Administration Staff". Pay type, one level down, is a fixed standard list (Regular, Overtime, Social Security, Medicare, and so on) and is the one consistent cross-org dimension available (10.9).

Regulated figures

Payroll totals feed W-2s, tax filings, and board reporting. The widget should signal how current its data is.

Rights and PII

Pay data is sensitive. Visibility follows user rights; any drill to per-employee detail must respect payroll-view permissions.

Company scoping

Company (with fiscal year, period, bank account) is global product context, set in the top-right switcher for the whole Accounting product, not a per-widget control. The widget reads whatever company is active there (verified). Company switch is global for the majority (SME, 8b). A widget-level scope (such as pay group) would be a genuinely new kind of control, not an extension of company scoping.

Accessibility (Pathway AA)

Amounts must be readable as text, not hover-only. The breakdown must be programmatically associated with its labels. Controls and any icon-only buttons must be named and keyboard-reachable. The current widget fails this on real data.

Plain language

Part-time non-specialist users. "Total paid", "Housing allowance", not codes or jargon.

Works within the widget, not new pages

For now, everything the widget offers (including any detail view) should live in the widget and its focus overlay, not spawn new module pages or reports.

10. Significant design decisions and trade-offs

This section records the decisions that shape the widget and, above all, why, so the reasoning is not lost. It names user needs and the evidence behind them. Where it mentions a possible solution, that is offered as an option with its rationale for the designer to decide, not as a spec.

10.1 The period control is the primary control, and it must serve two mindsets

Decision: the single most important control is how the user sets the period the total covers. As a user, I need to easily control the period I am looking at, or set my own custom range.

Why it is not the same as Deposits On Hand: Deposits On Hand shows a balance, which has a meaningful "as of today" value, so it can lead with today's number. Payroll Distributions shows a flow: money paid over a span. There is no single-day value (most days nobody is paid), so the total only means something across a window. The control therefore has to define a span, and the headline number is the sum across that span, not a latest value.

Two mindsets to serve (Section 5): operational users (P1) think by pay run ("did the last payroll look right"); reporting users (P2, P3) think in calendar and fiscal periods ("what have we spent this year"), which is also how boards, budgets, and tax filings are framed. The SME confirms both: pay-period thinking, "mostly by month", and a strong year-to-date orientation elsewhere in the same session. The period control needs to answer both without forcing either group into the other's vocabulary. Custom range must always be available (SME, hard requirement).

10.2 The hard trade-off: what unit should the period come in?

This is the crux, and it was worked through carefully because the obvious answer is wrong in a way that matters for a regulated figure. Three candidate units were considered.

Option

The idea

Why it falls short

A. Calendar spans (this month, this quarter, YTD, this year, custom)

Familiar units that match how boards, budgets, and tax filings are framed. Always well-defined regardless of how an org is structured

A calendar month does not always contain the same number of pay runs. Biweekly pay produces 26 periods a year, so two months each year carry a third paycheck (the documented "three-paycheck month"). This makes calendar windows unreliable for any period-over-period math, though that is now moot since comparison is out of scope (10.3). For simply displaying a chosen span's total, calendar spans are fine

B. Declared pay cycle (know the org is biweekly, count back)

Would be the most "native" framing, aligned to how payroll actually runs

Rejected. We cannot know an org's schedule in advance, and different pay groups within one org can run on different cadences, so there is no single "the pay cycle" at the company level

C. Retrospective pay dates (most recent pay date, last 3, last 6, read from history)

Needs no schedule prediction: the real check dates already sit in PRHistory (Section 2)

"The most recent pay date" is only well-defined if there is one. An org running a monthly church and a weekly daycare has no single last pay date, and "last 3 pay dates" could silently return three weekly daycare runs and omit the church entirely. Making it safe would require detecting the org's cadence structure, which we have no reliable way to do

Where this lands. The recommended direction is calendar-based spans (A) as the period unit, because they are well-defined for every org regardless of pay-group or cadence structure, they match the reporting mindset, and the always-available custom range covers the operational "exact pay run" case. The pay-date unit (C) was seriously considered but depends on cadence detection we cannot perform, and its main advantage (equal-event-count comparison) is no longer relevant now that comparison is out of scope (10.3).

10.3 Trend and comparison: rejected

Status: rejected for this widget. "Trend and comparison" means any element whose job is to express this period relative to another period: a trend line or mini-chart-over-time, a trend or delta badge ("+12% vs last month"), an up/down change arrow, or any "vs last period" figure, at any view including glance. None of these belong on this widget.

This is distinct from proportion, which is kept. Showing each line's share of the selected period's total (percent-of-total, relative bar length) is within-period and stays in scope; it is part of reading the breakdown, not trending it.

Why rejected:

  • Not worth the complexity. Doing period-over-period math correctly on payroll is hard: because of the three-paycheck-month effect (10.2), two calendar periods can contain different numbers of pay runs, so a naive comparison misleads on a regulated figure. Getting it right is disproportionate to the value.

  • Out of scope for this pass. No SME asked for comparison on this widget (the SME raised it for Budget-vs-Actual, not here); demand is inferred from general dashboard practice, not observed. It would need dedicated research and a solid use and business case before it is worth building.

The widget shows the selected period's own numbers, the grand total and the breakdown, and does not editorialize them against any other period. A concrete example of the kind of need that would reopen this: comparing one line (say PTO) for one pay group across two periods. That is a legitimate question, but it is exactly the period-over-period math this decision defers until there is a validated use and business case.

10.4 Pay group: deferred, not being brought in this pass

Decision: do not add a pay-group filter in this pass. The need is real, a multi-entity org (daycare / school / church) wanting to read one entity's distributions is a genuine, specific job raised by the SME (8b), but we cannot responsibly design the filter yet, because we do not understand the underlying model well enough (8c).

Why deferred: pay group is a grouping of employees, a different axis from the compensation / distribution / pay type chain the widget uses (10.9), and there is no clearly-labelled pay-group field on the employee record. More decisively, we do not know whether a historical distribution record even carries a pay-group key. Without that, every design question (one selector or several, how it composes with the date range, what it returns when an employee has changed groups) is unanswerable, and any filter risks producing wrong figures on regulated data. Establishing the model properly is a significant investigation into the payroll data model, needing Value Labs and schema access, not a quick check.

So: pay group is parked. It becomes worth doing when the multi-entity need is prioritised in its own right and the data-model investigation (8c) is funded. It is not a small add-on to this widget. If and when it is picked up, this page (8b, 8c) holds the need and the open questions to start from.

10.7 Shell dependency to raise

The canonical widget shell documents its period vocabulary as a fixed calendar list (Week / Month / Quarter / Fiscal year / Calendar year). Calendar spans (10.2) fit that vocabulary, so the recommended direction stays within the shell. Worth noting for the Figma / shell session: any future flow-type widget wanting a non-calendar cadence unit would need a shell change.

"Let me see it clearly, or two ways at once"

Same controls as detail, but full width gives room for complex charts to render with more clarity and for two data views side by side, for example the table and a donut together. Useful here because exact amounts (table) and proportion (donut) are both wanted, and seeing them together supports a board- or budget-prep read

10.9 The breakdown grain: distribution, with pay type as a possible second lens

Verified live (Compensation Information walk, 20 Jul 2026). The widget's table groups by distribution (the org-named, GL-mapped line, for example "AA Aid"). Underneath every distribution sits a pay type, a fixed standard classification (Regular, Overtime, Holiday, Vacation, Sick, Social Security, Medicare, and so on). See Section 1 for the full compensation / distribution / pay type hierarchy. This opens a design question about what the breakdown should be grouped by.

The trade-off between the two grains:

  • Distribution (today's grain) ties directly to the general ledger, so the numbers reconcile to what the bookkeeper sees in their accounts. The cost is that names are arbitrary per org, the list can get long, and it means nothing when comparing across orgs.

  • Pay type is standardized and meaningful: "how much went to overtime" or "how much employer tax (Social Security, Medicare)" is a real, consistent question. The cost is it is one step removed from the GL and collapses the org's own structure.

They answer different questions ("where did it post in my books" vs "what kind of pay was it"), and neither is strictly better.

Recommendation (to weigh, not a spec): keep distribution as the default grain (it preserves the GL reconciliation tie, which matters for a regulated figure and is today's behaviour), and add a "view by" grouping toggle offering pay type as the second lens. This mirrors the pattern already used on Deposits On Hand (all accounts / by type / individual), so it is consistent rather than novel. Within whichever grouping is shown, allow sort by amount (largest first), which is low-risk and serves the pulse-check and board-prep jobs. Do not replace distributions with pay types outright, that throws away the GL tie. Filtering to a single value is the weakest interaction (it collapses the breakdown that is the widget's point), so treat it as lower priority than the grouping toggle unless a specific need turns up.

Two caveats to resolve before committing:

  • To confirm, Value Labs. A pay-type grouping depends on the historical distribution record (PRHistoryCompensation) carrying the pay-type key per line. If it does, the second lens is cheap; if not, it is backend work. Same class of data-model question as pay group (8c).

  • To confirm, usage. How many distributions a typical org has decides whether the grouping toggle is worth it. A handful of distributions means the flat list is fine and the pay-type roll-up is over-engineering; dozens means the pay-type roll-up genuinely tames the noise. A Pendo or SME check should size this.

10.10 Pay-type breakdown drill-down (buildable, not in the widget today)

Source doc 23 Jul 2026 [DR] This is a proposed, buildable interaction, not current behaviour. The widget today has no drill-down (Sections 3 and 4): clicking a distribution opens nothing. The Shelby Financials technical analysis (23 Jul 2026) establishes that a full drill-down from a distribution down to individual employee checks and their pay-type split is supported by the existing database with no schema change. Stated plainly: it does not exist in the widget today, it is buildable.

The designed interaction. A user clicks a distribution row and the widget opens a detail panel (inside the widget or its focus overlay per 10.6, not a new page) showing, for that distribution and the selected date range: each employee and check (date, number), and within each, the pay-type breakdown (Regular, Overtime, Sick, and so on) with hours, rate and amount. This serves JTBD 5 (reconcile a line to detail) and the composition job (JTBD 2) at the pay-type grain. It also fits the drill-carries-filter model the SME says clients already love on reports (8b): the clicked distribution and the active date range carry into the detail as its filter, so the detail always reconciles to the row total.

Effort and worth. Medium effort, no schema change, data already exists, so impact is High for the payroll persona (P1 bookkeeper), who reconciles lines and answers "which employees or checks drove this" (scenario C). This aligns with the market baseline that a dashboard figure drills to detail in one click (Section 8) and with keeping detail inside the widget (10.6).

Pay-type breakdown drill-down — data feasibility (Shelby Financials analysis, Jul 2026)

Source doc 23 Jul 2026 Key facts, confirmed via the source document, not verified live:

  • Feasibility. A full breakdown of distribution amounts by pay type (Regular, Overtime, Sick, and so on) down to individual employee checks is supported by the DB. No schema change; only a new API endpoint plus UI drill-down.

  • Four-level hierarchy. (1) Total = SUM of PR_HistoryCompensation.Amount; (2) Per distribution = grouped by PR_CompensationDistribution.Name; (3) Per employee/check = PR_History (check date, number) plus PR_Employee (person); (4) Pay-type breakdown = PR_HistoryCompensation SubType plus Hours plus Rate plus Amount. Levels 2 and 4 come from the same table.

  • Current widget reads little of the table. Only Amount and CompensationDistributionID from PR_HistoryCompensation; Hours, Rate, SubType, PayCycle and others are ignored.

  • SubType (pay type) codes. 1 Regular, 2 Overtime, 3 Double Time, 4 Holiday, 5 Other, 6 Vacation, 7 Sick, 8 Personal, 9 Misc, 10 Other Pay. Codes 6 to 9 names are org-configurable via PR_Company (for example Vacation may be "PTO" or "Annual Leave") and must be resolved at query time.

  • Hourly vs salaried. Distinguished by a Classification field on the employee compensation record. Hourly: Amount = Hours x Rate, and Rate is the hourly rate. Salaried: Amount = period salary and Rate holds the same period-salary value (not an hourly rate), so an implied hourly rate must be calculated (salary / standard hours).

  • Exclusions to preserve so the drill-down matches top-level totals. Voided checks excluded (VoidJournalID IS NULL); manual checks excluded (CheckType = 2). Non-cash comp (health, allowances) is a separate table, PR_HistoryNonCash, and is not in distribution totals.

  • Optional per-line dimensions available. ProjectID (project-level breakdown possible later) and WorkersCompID (workers' comp class per line).

  • Proposed endpoint. GET /api/dashboard/payroll-distributions/breakdown?distributionName=...&startDate=...&endDate=... returning distributionName, dateRange, totalAmount, totalHours, employeeCount, and an employees[] array where each employee has name, checkDate, checkNumber, classification, totalAmount, totalHours, and a payBreakdown[] of {payType, subType, hours, rate, amount}.

  • Build work (no DB change). Modern API entity mapping (Low), new BreakdownDto (Low), new GetPayrollDistributionBreakdownAsync service method (Medium), new controller endpoint (Low), UI clickable distribution rows opening a per-employee/per-pay-type detail panel (Medium). Overall Medium effort, High value for the payroll persona.

10.11 Proposed design solution

Spec agreed 23 Jul 2026 The definite solution mirrors the Deposits On Hand pattern in Jo's Widget Container demo (draft v14), which already contains four Payroll Distributions mockups (all-distributions, by pay type, drilled-into-a-distribution, no-history). Single glance-to-detail widget; data feasibility per 10.10 (all fields in PR_HistoryCompensation, no schema change).

Controls (header).

  • Period range chip (for example "This year"). [O]

  • Grouping toggle "Distributions | Pay types": regroups the same period total by distribution (Pastoral Staff, Administration Staff, Worship & Music...) or by pay type (Regular Pay, Housing Allowance, Vacation, Overtime, Social Security, Sick...). [O] Both are standard competitor reports (labor distribution by department; earnings-type breakdown). [C]

  • View toggle "Table | Distribution" (donut); Trend can follow later as in Deposits. [O]

  • Glance KPI: large period total (demo $336,967). [O][DR]

All-distributions Table view.

  • Columns DISTRIBUTION | % OF TOTAL (ordered horizontal bar, descending) | AMOUNT plus a Total row. [O] Ordered bar with value as text (not pie, not hover-only) meets the pie-to-ordered-bar plus Pathway AA theme. [DR]

  • Every column sortable; each distribution row has a chevron to drill in. [O]

  • Production today lists distributions with total amounts (reads only Amount plus CompensationDistributionID); grouping toggle, pay-type view, sort and drill are the additions. [P]

Pay-types view. Same table regrouped by PAY TYPE (demo: Regular Pay 77%, Housing Allowance 7%, Vacation 6%, Overtime 4%, Social Security 4%, Sick 2%) = aggregate pay-type breakdown across all distributions. [O] Pay types = PR_HistoryCompensation.SubType 1-10 (Regular, Overtime, Double Time, Holiday, Other, Vacation, Sick, Personal, Misc, Other Pay); names 6-9 org-configurable. [DR]

Drill (single distribution). The distribution chevron opens a detail with a back breadcrumb "< All distributions"; subtitle "<Distribution>, all pay types for the selected period"; left = PAY TYPE | % OF TOTAL (ordered bar) | AMOUNT plus a Total row; right = a SYNCED donut (period total centred) with a full legend (each pay type amount plus %). Table and chart synced = the good model (contrast the unsynced AP/Loans/Receivables). [O][DR] Period and filter carry through the drill (drill-carries-filter, like Pension). [DR]

States and rules.

  • Empty (no history): "No payroll runs in this range" / "There's no payroll history for the selected period yet." [O]

  • Currency follows the org (avoid the per-widget GBP/USD localisation defect). [DR]

  • Exclusions to match totals: voided (VoidJournalID IS NULL) and manual checks (CheckType=2) excluded; non-cash comp (PR_HistoryNonCash) excluded. [DR - see 10.10]

Deeper grain (future). The same PR_HistoryCompensation join (employee via PR_History -> PR_Employee) supports a per-employee/per-check register under a distribution, matching the competitor payroll-register detail grain. [C][DR]

Effort and impact. Medium (no schema change; a new breakdown endpoint per 10.10 plus this UI). High value for the payroll persona. [DR]
