Part A. What exists today

1. What it is

Shows how many people are enrolled in each insurance plan, filterable by insurance type - a quick overview of plan uptake across the organisation. Despite the "Billing" name, it reports enrollment counts, not billing/premium amounts.

SME HQ / denominational, module-dependent; used for the Methodist-conference segment (~5% of clients, alongside pension billing), low traffic but important to those who use it (Marvin).

2. Where the data comes from

The Insurance Billing module. It counts the number of people enrolled in each plan - including both employees and their dependents, not just employees. Types come from the same module. Tables: IBPlan (plans, types), IBType (type categories), IBEmployeePlans (employee enrolments), IBEmployeeDependents (dependent enrolments). Prior doc, to confirm.

Part B. Users and market

5. Personas

Benefits administration. The conference / HQ insurance-benefits administrator is the primary user - managing plan enrolment. HR reads uptake; finance cares about the premium/billing cost behind enrolment; a church admin cares about their own staff's coverage. HQ-specific (e.g. Methodist conference) audience.

8. Competitor and analog benchmark

Desktop research Benefits-enrolment dashboards (ADP, Paycor, Selerix, benefits-admin tools) report more than a raw headcount: participation rate (enrolled vs eligible; medical >80% voluntary, dental/vision 60-80% are healthy), enrolment by plan and coverage tier, the employee-vs-dependent split, and - central for a billing context - cost/premium per plan (PEPM, employer vs employee contribution). For comparing a handful of plans a bar reads more precisely than a pie, and drilling to enrollees is standard. Sources: Selerix (participation benchmarks), Umbrex (enrolment by plan/tier, cost), ADP.

Part C. Designer brief

9. Target composition (what the widget should be built from)

The concrete make-up, so it can be built and demoed consistently. A floor, not a straitjacket. Triage: P current production (keep what works, delete nothing used), C competitor, SME Ben Lane, DR desktop/best practice, O Oisin's demo.

Controls (header):

  • Type dropdown (All Types default; Dental / Medical / Property / Vision - confirmed live 23 Jul 2026), syncing both views. Refresh with a "data as of" timestamp. [P]

  • Optional chart-type toggle (bar/pie). [DR]

Views:

  • Glance (KPI): total enrolled (with employee vs dependent split), number of plans, and - if available - participation rate and total premium/billing. [DR]

  • Breakdown: table (plan, enrolment count, employee/dependent split, and premium/cost if available) synced with a horizontal bar by plan (clearer than the pie). [P + DR]

  • Drill (add, for parity with Pension Plans): plan -> enrollees (name, church/org, tier) in a focus overlay. [DR; sibling parity]

Interaction & rules:

  • Resolve counts vs billing: decide whether a "Billing" widget should show premium/cost alongside (or instead of) headcount - the name sets an expectation the data doesn't meet today. [DR; naming]

  • Split employees vs dependents - the count bundles both; surfacing the split is more informative. [DR]

  • Preserve the synced table+chart. [P strength]

  • Values as text; named controls; keyboard. [accessibility]

The main metric (Number Enrolled) is a count, not currency, so the £/$ localisation defect does not apply to it (verified live 23 Jul 2026). Currency localisation only becomes relevant if premiums are added later. Values as text; named controls.

11. Significant design decisions

11.1 Show more than a headcount - add the employee/dependent split and, where available, participation rate; a raw count is thin. 11.2 Resolve counts vs billing - a "Billing" widget likely should surface premium/cost per plan; decide with product. 11.3 Add a drill to enrollees for parity with Pension Plans (and the obvious "who's on this plan?" need). 11.4 Bar over pie for comparison; keep the sync. 11.5 Accessibility parity - values as text, named controls. 11.6 Sibling consistency with Pension Plans - the two HQ billing widgets should behave the same way.
