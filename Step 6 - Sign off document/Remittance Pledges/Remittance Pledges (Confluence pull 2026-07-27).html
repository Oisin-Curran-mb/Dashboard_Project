Part A. What exists today

1. What it is

Shows how well the organisation is keeping up with its remittance pledge commitments - the regular obligations a church sends to a conference or denominational body (e.g. Methodist apportionments, mission funds). Per activity type: what was pledged for the year, what was expected by a given date, what has been paid, what is outstanding, and the percentage paid. Its distinctive value is pacing: are we keeping up with the year?

SME A module-dependent HQ / denominational widget (Remittance module); lower traffic, but central to the conferences/denominational bodies that use it - the same ~5% Methodist-conference segment as pension/insurance billing (Marvin).

2. Where the data comes from

The Remittance module, scoped to the current org and filtered by a "Date Receipts Thru" date (only payments up to that date count). The "Percent of year completed" figure is computed on the fly from days elapsed in the calendar year to the selected date. Table: RMActivityRepository (pledge amounts, payments, outstanding by activity type). Prior doc, to confirm.

Part B. Users and market

5. Personas

Denominational-obligation-centric. The church treasurer / bookkeeper pays apportionments and tracks pace against the year. Conference / denominational HQ finance monitors whether churches are keeping up. The pastor / board read it as an accountability/transparency obligation. This is the HQ/denominational (e.g. Methodist conference) audience.

7. Context scenarios

A. Treasurer checks whether the church is on pace on its apportionments. B. Year-end - catch up before the January remittance deadline (unpaid carries forward). C. Conference/HQ monitors a church's remittance progress. D. Board/accountability report on obligations met. E. As-of-date status for a finance report.

8. Competitor and analog benchmark

Desktop research Denominational apportionment/remittance tracking is niche (Methodist conferences, dioceses; church accounting suites). Key domain facts that shape the design: apportionments are usually remitted monthly (so a straight-line "expected" is only an approximation of the real schedule); they run on the calendar year (payments due by early January for prior-year credit) - which validates a calendar-based "% of year completed"; and unpaid amounts carry forward to the next year, so year-end catch-up matters. Conferences publish remittance reports so churches can track their pace. The relevant analog pattern is obligation pacing: paid vs expected-by-now, with an on-track/behind read. Sources: UMC Church Law (monthly remittance, ¶617), NC Conference remittances, Florida Conference (carry-forward), UMC.org (formula).

Part C. Designer brief

9. Target composition (what the widget should be built from)

The concrete make-up, so it can be built and demoed consistently. A floor, not a straitjacket. Triage: P current production (keep what works, delete nothing used), C competitor, SME Ben Lane, DR desktop/best practice, O Oisin's demo.

Controls (header):

  • Receipts through (Date Receipts Thru) date picker, default today, persists once Refreshed (verified live 23 Jul 2026); keep "Percent of year completed" as the pacing reference. Refresh with a "data as of" timestamp. [P]

Views:

  • Glance (KPI): total annual, total paid, % paid vs % of year elapsed (the pacing read) with an on-track/behind indicator, total outstanding, and days-to-year-end context. [DR pacing]

  • Progress/pacing per activity: a bar of paid vs annual with a marker at "expected by now"; colour on-track vs behind. [DR]

  • Table: Seq., Activity, Annual, YTD Expected, YTD Paid, Outstanding, % Paid + totals (all columns verified live 23 Jul 2026), plus an explicit on-track / behind status column. [P + DR]

Interaction & rules:

  • Surface pacing status - don't make the user compare % Paid to % year completed by eye; compute on-track/behind per activity and overall. [DR]

  • Drill an activity -> payment history (and highlight behind-schedule ones for year-end catch-up). [today none]

  • Clarify "YTD Expected": it is straight-line today; if the real remittance cadence is monthly, expected should follow that schedule. [DR monthly cadence]

  • Confirm and label the year basis (calendar for apportionments), don't force org fiscal year here. [DR]

  • Values as text; named controls; keyboard. [accessibility]

11. Significant design decisions

11.1 Make pacing the headline. Compute and show on-track/behind (% paid vs % year elapsed) rather than leaving the user to compare two numbers - this is the widget's distinctive job. 11.2 Add a progress/pacing visual per activity (paid vs annual with an expected-by-now marker). 11.3 Glance KPI - annual/paid/outstanding + overall pacing + year-end context. 11.4 Get "expected" right - if remittances are monthly, straight-line expected mis-states "behind"; match the real cadence. 11.5 Confirm/label the year basis (calendar for apportionments; do not force org fiscal year). 11.6 Add drill to payment history and highlight behind-schedule activities for year-end catch-up. 11.7 Sibling consistency with Gifts Pledges (date-persistence behaviour confirmed identical, 23 Jul 2026). 11.8 Accessibility parity.
