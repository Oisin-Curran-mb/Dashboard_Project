1. What Deposits On Hand is

Verified live Deposits On Hand is the module behind the widget. A deposit account is money the organization holds on behalf of a depositor (a person or organization from Records). The structure is a three-level hierarchy:

Level

What it is

Example from asmdev

Group

Top-level grouping of account types

NW Group

Account Type

Category with its own interest settings; populates the widget filter

NW Group/Jo's Account Type 1

Account

One depositor's account: name, number, inception date, balance, active status

Nicole whatnot, #123456

Verified live An account's lifecycle: created and edited in Modify > Account Information; money moves via Enter > Transaction (deposit, withdrawal, adjustment); withdrawals generate a check in Accounts Payable; interest is calculated and posted in batch by Utilities > Post Interest Deposits; closing happens through a Close Account dialog that posts final interest through a chosen date; depositor statements are configured in Modify > Statement Design.

Desktop research Who runs this in the real world: denominational headquarters and church extension funds, where congregations and individuals deposit money that the body pays interest on and lends to affiliated churches. These are regulated securities programs, not FDIC-insured bank accounts. This makes the widget a small portfolio view over depositor money, a different thing from the Bank Balances widget (operational cash).

The role of account type. Desktop research Type is the product line of a deposit program: interest settings live at type level, Post Interest runs off type-driven accrual settings, and real extension funds structure offerings this way (demand investments, fixed-rate term certificates, IRA-type accounts at LCEF and Disciples CEF). For the HQ administrator, type is the portfolio dimension (how much sits in each product, where interest liability concentrates) and account is the depositor dimension (who called, what is their balance).

Naming: the widget says "Deposit Accounts", the module says "Deposits On Hand". Same thing, two names, on the same screen. Verified live Decision on the table: rename the widget to match the module (Section 8, Gap 9).

3. The module today: no read layer

Verified live Walked the full module to test where a widget drill-down could land. Finding: there is nowhere to land. Unlike Accounts Payable (View > Transaction Inquiry), Deposits On Hand has no View menu, no live activity table, no interest history, no list of closed accounts. Everything is entry, edit, action, or report:

In practice: "which accounts have unposted interest" = run a Columnar or Detail report in the separate reports app. "Which accounts closed recently" = search Account Information with Show Only Inactive, one account at a time. "Did the payout go through" = leave the module and find the check in Accounts Payable.

4. Personas

Desktop research Deposits On Hand is a headquarters feature, which inverts our usual persona hierarchy: the part-time church admin is secondary here.

Persona

Relationship to this widget

P1. HQ finance administrator (primary)

Staff accountant at a denominational HQ, district office, or extension fund. Manages depositor accounts, posts interest, processes withdrawals via AP, answers depositor questions, reports to leadership and regulators.

P2. Part-time church admin or bookkeeper (secondary)

Often part time (a real posting: 20 hrs/week at $20/hr) or volunteer, no formal accounting training. Touches this widget only if the org runs deposit accounts, or misreads it as bank balances.

P3. Treasurer / finance committee (oversight)

High-capacity volunteer doing review, not data entry. Needs totals and trend for board reporting and anomaly spotting.

P4. Depositor-facing service staff (contextual)

At extension funds, staff field depositor calls about balances, rates, maturities. The account grid is their fastest lookup.

5.2 Context scenarios

Desktop research, grounded in the verified module map. Each ends with how it plays out today.

A. Monday morning at a district office. The HQ administrator opens the dashboard, sees the total and type mix. One type looks lower than last week. Today: no comparison exists, so "lower" depends on memory; investigating means a report in the separate reports app.

B. Depositor phone call. A congregation treasurer asks their balance and when interest last posted. Today: staff can scan the grid for the balance if the list is short; the interest question requires a Detail report. No search, no click-through.

C. Quarter-end interest run. The administrator wants to know which accounts accrue this quarter and whether anything is unposted before running Post Interest. Today: nothing signals this; the Columnar report is the only pre-check.

D. Board packet week. The treasurer needs deposits by type, trend vs last quarter, and totals readable by non-accountants. Today: type totals exist only in the pie (hover to read); trend does not exist; the packet gets built from reports.

E. Small church edge case. A part-time admin at a church that does not use Deposits On Hand selects the widget expecting bank balances and gets an empty grid. Today: "No records to display" with blank totals, no explanation, no way forward.

5.4 Constraints

Constraint

What it means for the design

Rights and PII

Accounts carry depositor names and balances. Per-account visibility follows user rights (Bank Balances precedent). No account data in states visible to unauthorized users.

Segregation of duties

Custody, recording, and review are separate roles in church finance. The widget stays read-and-navigate; create and edit actions appear only behind rights (see state 3).

Regulatory accuracy

Balances feed depositor statements and extension-fund investor reporting (NASAA-regulated). Every figure needs a visible "data as of" stamp and must reconcile to the module.

Account identity

Two accounts can share the same name and be different accounts, distinguished only by account number (verified with the product owner). Any list, drill, or comparison must keep same-named accounts unambiguous (see Gap 14).

Org-driven formatting

Currency and dates come from the organization's locale, never the client's or server's. The live audit rendered £ and dd/mm/yyyy on a US product (Gap 12).

Plain language

Volunteers and part-timers run this market. Labels like "Total on deposit" and "Interest not yet posted", never module jargon.

Accessibility

WCAG 2.1 AA per the Pathway Accessibility Gate. All values available as text (no hover-only data), one semantic table with header association, labeled controls, keyboard-reachable tooltip.

Framework reality

The dashboard is ASP.NET Web Forms: two widget slots, full postbacks, panels swapped via the title link. Interactive patterns cost more here; a full-width view may be framework work.

Reports live in a separate application

Any "open report" link leaves Financials. Label such links honestly ("Opens in Reports") and treat the transition as a designed moment.

Legacy continuity

Long-tenured users report navigation confusion after past changes. Renames and layout changes need continuity cues (subtitle, tooltip, unchanged data meaning).

6. Competitor and analog benchmark

Desktop research No competitor has a "deposits on hand" widget by that name; the closest analogs are account-balance widgets. The bar they set: balance + status + one-click action into a live screen. Their drill-downs work because a live screen already sits behind the card; Section 3 shows we lack that screen. Scale also differs: Xero and QBO list 3 to 10 bank accounts per org, while a deposit program holds hundreds to thousands of depositor accounts (Disciples CEF alone: 2,000+ individual and 800+ congregation investors), which is why the account grain does not fit our dashboard the way it fits theirs.

7. Design direction: three depth levels

Grain decision. The widget's dashboard grain is the account type (one row per type: name, # accounts, total balance, share), not the individual account. An org has a handful of types but hundreds to thousands of accounts, so only the type grain fits a glance; type is where interest settings and portfolio thinking live (Section 1); and a text type-summary replaces both the flat account list and the hover-only pie, resolving Gaps 1 and 5 in one component. Individual accounts move to L3 behind a click, with a Type column and search. Note the account grid must keep accounts distinguishable when two share a name: the current widget relies on its Account# column for this, so the L3 grid must retain the account number (see Gap 14).

7.1 Time range and chart axes

Deposits On Hand shows a balance, not a flow. A balance has a meaningful value on any given day (money sitting on deposit right now), unlike Payroll Distributions where the headline is a sum of what moved over a span. That one difference decides how time works here and what the axes are, and it makes this widget behave in the opposite way to the Payroll one.

The Y axis is always the balance amount (total on deposit, in the organization's currency). It does not change with the time range or the view. Whatever span or granularity is chosen, the vertical axis is money on deposit.

The X axis, and what "time range" means, depends on which of two views the widget is in.

View

What the time range does

X axis

Y axis

Snapshot (composition), the L1 total and L2 by-type mix

Sets the as-of date (default today). A single point in time, no trend

Account type (the bars or segments of the mix)

Balance as of that date

Trend (over time), the L3 balance history

Sets the window shown

Time, plotted as end-of-period points

Balance as of each period end (not a sum of activity in the period)

Because a balance only steps when money moves (deposits, withdrawals, and above all the batch interest posting), the natural X granularity for the trend is the fiscal period (or month), not days. A daily balance line for a deposit fund would be mostly flat with occasional steps; period-end points carry the signal without the noise.

Quarter (this or last) is a reasonable secondary span for the board cadence, but a quarter holds only about three period points, a thin trend, so I would treat it as optional rather than core.

On fiscal period specifically. For a balance widget, fiscal period never goes on the Y axis; Y is always the balance. Fiscal period belongs on the X axis as the tick interval for the trend (one point per period end) and as the anchor for an as-of snapshot ("balance as of end of Period 6"). It fits this widget well because it is already first-class product context (the top-right switcher runs on fiscal year and period) and because interest posts on that cadence, so period ends are exactly where balances step. So fiscal-aligned spans belong in the list, and fiscal period is the natural granularity, not a quantity on the Y axis.

Compare-to options for MVP. Under the trend, the "compare to" selector (the baseline the current balance is measured against to produce the change figure, delta, or trend line) ships for MVP with these items: previous week, previous month, previous quarter, previous fiscal year, and previous calendar year. These are deliberately simple and standard. This is new functionality, the data layer is still unclear, and there is not yet enough research to justify anything more elaborate, so MVP keeps the baseline set small and conventional; feedback over time will guide what to adjust or add to make it more usable in future.

The gate (same shape as the pay-group question on the Payroll widget). Every trend span assumes we can obtain historical period-end balances. The live audit (Section 3) found the module has no history surface: balances are computed as of today, and movement and interest history live only in the separate reports application. So a balance-over-time trend depends on the data layer being able to reconstruct period-end balances from the deposit transaction history (DHAccount), or it is a data-layer build. The snapshot spans (as of a date) are cheap: the existing calculation already produces an as-of-today balance and could likely produce an as-of-any-date one. The trend spans are the ones that need the data-model check, worth confirming with engineering before committing the trend to a near-term release.

Trend and comparison are wanted here, unlike on Payroll Distributions. A balance drifts continuously and leadership asks which way it is going, so a trend and a delta against the prior period earn their place (Gap 4, JTBD 4 and 5). On Payroll the number is a periodic flow where period-over-period math misleads, which is why it was rejected there. The two widgets differ because a balance and a flow differ.

9. Top 10 insights

  1. Design for the HQ administrator first. Headquarters module, so P1 leads and the part-time church admin is secondary. Inverted from our usual hierarchy.

  2. The widget's job is portfolio oversight plus fast lookup. Glance-and-drill, which fits KPI-plus-grid.

  3. The dashboard grain is the account type, not the account. A handful of types fits a glance; hundreds of depositor accounts do not. Type summary at L2, account grid at L3.

  4. Status beats data. An interest-cycle badge turns the widget into an early-warning system; today that signal exists only inside reports.

  5. Drill-down needs a destination, and it does not exist. Matching the market means building the L3 inquiry surface, not adding links. Interim links labeled honestly ("Opens in Reports").

  6. Every number needs a comparison. Delta and trend, or the balance answers nothing.

  7. Trust markers are a feature. Data-as-of stamp, org-driven currency and dates, interest-posted state. The live audit found failures in all three.

  8. Plain language everywhere. Volunteers and part-timers run this market; no module jargon in labels.

  9. Rights and duties in the widget. Account visibility per user rights; read-and-navigate only.

  10. Fix the chart and the grid semantics. Keep the type mix, lose the hover-only pie, rebuild the split tables into one semantic table.

10. Open questions and next steps

  • Module entitlement detection (state 1): how does the platform know an org has Deposits On Hand, and can the widget chooser filter on it? Engineering to confirm.

  • Widget rights behavior (state 2): does widget visibility respect module security rights today?

  • Rename check (Gap 9): audit where "Deposit Accounts" appears customer-facing (reports app, statements, training) before renaming.

  • Localization root cause (Gap 12): org setting, server culture, or browser culture? US customers must always see $ and US dates.

  • Historical balances for the trend (7.1): can the data layer reconstruct period-end balances from the deposit transaction history (DHAccount), or is a balance-over-time trend a data-layer build? This gates the trend time-spans; the snapshot (as-of-date) spans do not depend on it.

  • L3 scope decision: build the inquiry surface, or ship interim deep links to Account Information and pre-filtered reports?

  • Can the reports app accept pre-filled criteria via URL, so links land on a ready-to-run report instead of a blank form?

  • Interest status (due, unposted): is the data model ready to expose it outside reports?

  • Does the dashboard framework support a full-width widget, or is that framework work?

  • Pendo pull: what share of customers use Deposits On Hand, and how often is this widget selected?

  • Interviews: 3 to 5 headquarters customers; do they field depositor calls from this screen or from reports?

  • Follow-up audit pass: visual layer (contrast, spacing, focus indicators, reflow) and realistic data volumes (pagination, performance).

  • Validation: 5-user task test on an L1/L2/L3 prototype covering lookup, status check, and drill.
